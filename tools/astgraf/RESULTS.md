### Notes that matter more than the numbers

**#8, #9 and #10 each closed an excuse.** Before them it could fairly be said
that (a) the doctrine had never been tested on held-out data, (b) flood rules
had only ever been graded against an earthquake catalogue, and (c) the
author's headline rule had been scored against just 31 episodes. All three
are now spent.

**#10 is the only one that is merely underpowered rather than refuted.** Its
whole result rests on **12 firings against 7.0 expected — a 1.4 σ excess**.
The predicate is rare (0.84% of events), so a 1,435-event corpus still yields
single digits. If the 1.80 lift were real: n ≈ 3,000 → 2.3 σ, n ≈ 6,000 →
3.2 σ, n ≈ 12,000 → 4.6 σ. **This one is decidable with data alone.**

**Superseded scoring.** The band trigger's earlier figures — grid 0/31 vs
0.63 expected, proximity 1/31 vs 1.79, against `NATURAL DISASTERS.xlsx` — are
retained for the record but carry essentially no power at n = 31. #10

## Part 3 — Structural results (not statistical)

Facts about what the doctrine's own construction can and cannot express.
These do not depend on any catalogue.

- **The declination ceiling.** A sub-planet point's latitude is its body's
  declination. Measured achieved extremes 1900–2030: Jupiter +23.52°, Saturn
  +22.84°, Uranus +23.71°, Neptune +22.39°. The located set therefore caps at
  **23.71°N**, and **44.4%** of M7+ epicenters lie beyond it — Gorkha (28.23°N)
  among them. Restoring the Moon's missing latitude would lift the ceiling to
  +28.58° and still leave 38.5% unreachable — and the Moon's declination at
  the Nepal instant was only **+15.94°**, so even that escape hatch fails on
  the flagship case.
- **The canon has no lunar latitude.** `ASTGRAF.BAS` accumulates twenty terms
  into `ML` and ends `PX = LL + ML` — a longitude series with no `sin(F)`
  latitude term anywhere. The port reproduces this faithfully. The ceiling is
  the author's own tool's, not our port's.
- **A slow–slow crossing cannot define a meridian.** Crossings of slow pairs
  last days; meridians sweep 360° per day. Geometric longitude can therefore
  only come from a fast body — and every fast-body channel (#4, #5) is
  graded null. The geometric location route is exhausted **by derivation**,
  not merely by testing.
- **Three longitude mechanisms, one latitude mechanism.** Rotation, ecliptic
  tilt and light travel all move longitude; Earth's spin cannot move latitude
  at all. Latitude has exactly one source — declination — and it is capped.
- **The 1/81 cell is a resolution, not an origin.** It says how finely you may
  cut; a subdivision still needs a zero to cut from.
- **The author's ground distances run ~11% optimistic.** 1° at the equator is
  111.319 km, not 100; the equator moves 465.1 m/s, not 400; the 1/81° cell is
  1,374 m, not 1,234. His angular arithmetic is otherwise exact
  (240 s ÷ 81 = 2.963 s).

## Part 5 — How these results were produced

The method rulings that make the scoreboard trustworthy, each earned by a
mistake this project actually made:

1. **Pre-register cell, direction and statistic before running**, and commit
   the pre-registration before the result exists. (Verifiable in git: the
   cell-region design was committed 13:55:27 and its result 14:00:45, with an
   empty diff between.)
2. **Every null needs a power check.** Earned when a broken power arm reported
   the family max instead of the planted cell.
3. **Replication is not confirmation.** The dwell finding survived three kill
   attempts and two independent replications and was still false.
4. **Held-out sets must be strictly disjoint.** M6+ contains M7+; the test
   used M6.0–6.99.
5. **Controls must match the catalogue's own biases.** Uniform controls once
   promoted an era-locked predicate to lift 55 through nothing but the
   catalogue's completeness gradient; a uniform time grid once faked p = 0.000
   through a 3:8 lunar commensurability.
6. **Honest negatives stay on the record, with their numbers.**
